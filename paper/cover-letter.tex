\subsection*{Why this work fits the journal}

Intelligent systems increasingly consume probabilistic machine-learning
output, yet a probability triple does not by itself say whether a case is safe
to act on automatically. A forecast of $(0.36, 0.32, 0.32)$ and one of
$(0.78, 0.14, 0.08)$ can share a well-defined argmax while warranting
completely different operational responses. Our manuscript builds and
evaluates an intelligent decision layer that answers that second question:
when should a learned system act, when should it hand a case to review, and
when should it abstain? We believe this is a natural fit for
\textit{Expert Systems with Applications}, because the contribution is a
working intelligent system---probability estimation, uncertainty
quantification, and decision routing---evaluated under realistic constraints
rather than as an isolated algorithmic benchmark.

\subsection*{Core contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A market-anchored admission gate.} Candidate models are combined
with a strong external probabilistic prior through a convex weight selected
strictly before the target period. Because zero weight is admissible, a
learned model is \emph{rejected} from the probability layer when it does not
add information. On a complete held-out season, five-seed XGBoost and
random-forest ensembles receive essentially zero weight (mean 0.004; 11 of 12
rolling season--seed runs select exactly zero).

\item \textbf{Match-day adaptive class-conditional conformal triage.}
Class-specific conformal thresholds are updated only after every match on a
date has been resolved, so no outcome can influence a prediction made on the
same day. Prediction-set size then defines an explicit three-action interface:
singleton $\rightarrow$ automated forecast, doubleton $\rightarrow$ review,
full set $\rightarrow$ abstention. Across four rolling target seasons,
adaptation reduces mean absolute coverage error from 0.95 to 0.14 percentage
points and the mean class-coverage gap from 4.40 to 0.67 points relative to
fixed class-conditional calibration, while also reducing mean set size.

\item \textbf{A module-admission audit rather than a model showcase.} Our
protocol combines a complete-season refresh, five random seeds, exact paired
tests, and league-by-week block bootstrap intervals. Within it, an existing
1,104-match large-language-model run is tested conditionally on the cases
routed to review or abstention; it improves nothing in any stratum. We report
that negative result as evidence rather than hiding it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Evidence and rigor}

The study covers 12,459 matches from the top divisions of England, Spain,
Germany, Italy and France over seven seasons, including the \emph{complete}
2025/26 season as a 1,752-match final period. No threshold, weight, or
hyperparameter is tuned on the test period. The pipeline is script-driven and
deterministic: repeated runs of identical code reproduce all reported
quantities exactly, and every result can be regenerated from public raw data
(football-data.co.uk and Understat).

We also state the boundary of our claims plainly. We do not propose a new
conformal coverage theorem and we do not claim to have invented conformal
prediction or human-in-the-loop triage. The contribution is the market-anchored
triage framework, the sequential class-conditional controller, and the pairing
of module admission with out-of-sample evidence. Our theoretical statements are
correspondingly limited to empirical prequential coverage rather than
distribution-free guarantees.
