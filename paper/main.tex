\begin{abstract}
Forecasting systems are usually judged by whether their point predictions
beat a benchmark, although a decision system must also determine when a
prediction is too ambiguous to automate. This study develops a
market-anchored adaptive conformal framework for three-way football outcome
forecasting. A validation-selected convex combination allows a learned model
to contribute only when it improves on de-vigged closing-market probabilities.
Class-conditional conformal thresholds are then updated after each match day,
and prediction-set size routes a case to automatic prediction, review, or
abstention. The framework is evaluated on 12,459 matches from five major
European leagues over seven seasons, including the complete 2025/26 season as
a 1,752-match final period. Five-seed XGBoost and random-forest ensembles do
not significantly improve on the market; the selected learned-model weight is
nearly zero across four rolling target seasons. At a 90\% target coverage on
2025/26, the adaptive class-conditional method attains 89.8\% empirical
coverage, with home/draw/away coverage of 90.0/89.7/89.5\%. It automatically
resolves 15.7\% of matches at 74.9\% accuracy, routes 31.8\% to review, and
abstains on 52.5\%. Across four target seasons, adaptation reduces mean
absolute target-coverage error from 0.95 to 0.14 percentage points and the
mean class-coverage gap from 4.40 to 0.67 points relative to fixed
class-conditional calibration. An audit of an existing 1,104-match large
language model run finds no incremental accuracy in any triage stratum. The
results support a restrained role for artificial intelligence: risk-controlled
routing is reliable even when additional predictors do not beat an efficient
external prior.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} conformal prediction; selective forecasting;
algorithmic triage; probability calibration; football analytics; large
language models; market efficiency

\section{Introduction}

Probabilistic forecasts enter decisions through two distinct questions. The
first asks which outcome is most likely. The second asks whether the available
evidence is sufficiently decisive for an automated action. Most sports
forecasting studies emphasize the first question, reporting classification
accuracy, proper scoring rules, or simulated betting returns. A user of the
forecast nevertheless faces the second question on every match. A probability
triple such as $(0.36,0.32,0.32)$ and one such as $(0.78,0.14,0.08)$ should not
produce the same operational response, even if both have a well-defined
argmax.

Football is a demanding environment for studying this distinction. Three-way
outcomes are noisy, draws are difficult to discriminate, and closing odds
aggregate information from many market participants. Learned models may match
the market while adding little independent signal. This setting exposes a
weakness in conventional selective prediction: ranking cases by maximum model
confidence can identify easy favourites, but it neither controls class-wise
coverage nor establishes that an accepted prediction is useful relative to
the market. It also creates a stringent test for large language models (LLMs).
An LLM can return polished probabilities from a structured feature card, yet
the relevant question is whether it contributes information on the cases that
another system already identifies as ambiguous.

The present study converts football forecasting from unconditional point
classification into a risk-controlled routing problem. The proposed pipeline
first treats de-vigged closing odds as an external probabilistic anchor. A
learned model is combined with that anchor through a convex weight selected
strictly before the target period. The resulting probabilities are converted
to class-conditional conformal prediction sets. Set size then has an explicit
decision meaning: a singleton supports an automated point forecast, a
two-label set is sent for review, and a three-label set triggers abstention.
To address seasonal drift, the class-specific thresholds are updated only
after all matches on a date have been resolved. This prequential rule avoids
within-day outcome leakage while allowing recent errors to alter subsequent
coverage.

The empirical design is intentionally difficult to pass. Data cover 12,459
matches in the English, Spanish, German, Italian, and French top divisions
from 2019/20 through the complete 2025/26 season. Model comparisons use five
random seeds, exact paired tests, and league-by-week block bootstrap intervals.
Conformal results use four rolling target seasons, with outcome training,
early stopping, blend selection, calibration, and testing separated in time.
The LLM analysis reuses an existing 1,104-match run and asks whether its choices
improve the market within each conformal triage stratum; no additional API
queries are needed.

The contribution is methodological and evaluative rather than a new conformal
coverage theorem. First, the market anchor supplies a disciplined mechanism
for admitting or rejecting incremental model information. Second, a
match-day adaptive Mondrian controller combines class balance with sequential
drift response and converts set size into an operational three-action
interface. Third, the experiment links module admission to paired out-of-sample
evidence: neither a tabular model nor an LLM is retained merely because it is
nominally an AI component. Finally, the complete-season refresh demonstrates
why financial conclusions based on a season in progress are unstable; the
positive returns in the earlier partial sample disappear when the season is
completed.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Football forecasts and market benchmarks}

Poisson score models remain a standard starting point for association
football forecasting \cite{maher1982,dixon1997}. Elo ratings provide a compact
dynamic measure of team strength \cite{hvattum2010}, while random forests and
gradient-boosted trees represent common tabular machine-learning alternatives
\cite{breiman2001,chen2016}. Event-derived variables such as expected goals
enrich the feature set, but their incremental contribution must be assessed
against a strong market reference rather than against historical frequencies
alone \cite{decroos2019,robberechts2021}.

Fixed-odds markets have therefore served both as predictors and as tests of
forecast value. Goddard and Asimakopoulos \cite{goddard2004} studied English
league results and fixed-odds efficiency; comparisons of tipsters, betting
odds, and statistical forecasts similarly find that odds are difficult to
improve consistently \cite{spann2009,levitt2004,franck2010}. Closing odds are especially demanding
because they incorporate information arriving between market opening and
kickoff. The present study uses their de-vigged probabilities as an explicit
anchor and settlement reference. This design does not imply that closing odds
are executable at arbitrary scale; it isolates predictive information under a
common counterfactual protocol.

\subsection{Selective prediction and conformal sets}

Classification with a reject option formalizes the trade-off between coverage
and error on accepted cases \cite{chow1970,elyaniv2010}. Modern selective
classification methods learn or derive selection functions for neural and
other predictors \cite{geifman2017,geifman2019}. Confidence thresholding is
simple, but it does not directly guarantee that the true label belongs to a
reported candidate set.

Conformal prediction constructs sets calibrated to a target error rate under
exchangeability \cite{vovk2005}. Classification extensions include adaptive
prediction sets and regularized adaptive prediction sets
\cite{romano2020,angelopoulos2021}. Marginal coverage can conceal poor
performance for an outcome class, which motivates Mondrian or group-conditional
calibration. Probability calibration, ensemble uncertainty, and their failure
under distribution shift provide complementary diagnostics
\cite{guo2017,lakshminarayanan2017,ovadia2019}. Under covariate or temporal
shift, fixed conformal calibration may drift away from
its target; adaptive conformal inference uses feedback from observed errors to
update the effective miscoverage level
\cite{tibshirani2019,gibbs2021}. The method below
combines these established ideas in a temporally separated, class-conditional
football decision system. Because arbitrary temporal dependence violates the
usual exchangeability premise, the paper reports empirical prequential
coverage rather than claiming distribution-free validity across seasons.

Prediction sets have also been proposed as interfaces for human decision
support: the set can constrain or guide an expert rather than simply replace
one \cite{mozannar2020,straitouri2023}. Here no human-expert labels are available, so
``review'' is evaluated as workload routed away from automation rather than as
a simulated human improvement. An LLM is tested as one possible reviewer, and
its inclusion is determined by paired evidence.

\subsection{LLMs as probabilistic forecasters}

LLMs can produce probability forecasts when supplied with structured or
retrieved evidence \cite{halawi2024}. Retrieval-augmented, multi-agent, and
benchmark-arena systems extend this capability \cite{alur2025,yang2025}, but
they make timestamp control and information provenance more difficult. In
sports forecasting, a language model may also
copy the market probabilities supplied in its prompt. The appropriate test is
therefore incremental: does the LLM change errors relative to the anchor,
particularly on ambiguous cases? This study evaluates that question directly
instead of treating fluent probability output as independent reasoning.

\section{Problem Formulation}

Let $x_t$ denote the information available before match $t$, and let
$y_t\in\{H,D,A\}$ denote the home-win, draw, or away-win outcome. A forecasting
model produces $p_f(y\mid x_t)$, while de-vigged closing odds produce market
probabilities $p_m(y\mid x_t)$. A conventional point forecast is
\begin{equation}
  \hat y_t=\arg\max_{c\in\{H,D,A\}}p(c\mid x_t).
\end{equation}
The objective here is instead to return a set
$\Gamma_t(x_t)\subseteq\{H,D,A\}$ whose size controls the action:
\begin{equation}
 a_t=\begin{cases}
 \text{automate}, & |\Gamma_t|=1,\\
 \text{review}, & |\Gamma_t|=2,\\
 \text{abstain}, & |\Gamma_t|=3.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:triage}
\end{equation}
The risk-control target is class-conditional coverage near $1-\alpha$:
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\{y_t\in\Gamma_t(x_t)\mid y_t=c\}\approx1-\alpha,
 \qquad c\in\{H,D,A\}.
 \label{eq:classcoverage}
\end{equation}
The approximation sign is important. Exact finite-sample conformal guarantees
require exchangeability, whereas football observations are ordered and team
strength changes. Evaluation therefore emphasizes chronological generalization,
class-wise empirical coverage, and coverage drift.

Four research questions organize the experiments:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item Do learned tabular models add probability information beyond the closing
 market on a complete held-out season?
 \item Does class-conditional calibration correct the outcome imbalance hidden
 by marginal coverage?
 \item Does match-day adaptive feedback keep coverage closer to its target
 across seasons without inflating prediction sets?
 \item Does an LLM improve decisions in the cases routed to review or
 abstention?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data and Forecasting Models}

\subsection{Data and temporal construction}

The dataset contains 12,459 league matches from the English Premier League
(E0), Spanish La Liga (SP1), German Bundesliga (D1), Italian Serie A (I1), and
French Ligue 1 (F1), covering 2019/20--2025/26. Match results and opening and
closing odds are taken from the public football-data.co.uk files. Expected
goals, non-penalty expected goals, pressing intensity, deep progressions, and
expected points are matched from Understat. After name normalization, 99.3\%
of home teams and 99.2\% of away teams match the corresponding league-match
date.

All variables obey a pre-match cutoff. Five-match rolling statistics are
shifted by one match; standings and ranks are computed immediately before
kickoff; referee history uses only previous matches; missing-value medians and
normalization quantities are fitted on the relevant training window. A
verification script recomputes sampled features from raw records. The fixed
complete-season benchmark uses 2019/20--2023/24 for training (8,955 matches),
2024/25 for validation (1,752), and complete 2025/26 for testing (1,752).

The 60-dimensional representation comprises rolling goals, points, shots,
shots on target, corners, and fouls; season-cumulative points and goal
difference; league rank; rolling xG-derived quantities; referee history where
available; and eleven market variables derived from opening and closing odds,
movement, and cross-bookmaker dispersion. Referee coverage is limited to the
Premier League, which bounds conclusions about that feature group.

\subsection{Forecasting baselines}

The market baseline inverts and renormalizes closing 1X2 odds. Statistical
baselines are a Poisson score model and Elo ratings mapped to three-class
probabilities through multinomial logistic regression. Machine-learning
baselines are a 500-tree random forest and XGBoost with 500 boosting rounds,
depth six, learning rate 0.05, row and column subsampling of 0.8, and early
stopping on validation log loss. Five random seeds quantify stochastic
variation; their mean probabilities form the ensemble used for paired tests.

The existing LLM experiment uses DeepSeek-Chat with a fixed pre-match feature
card, no retrieval, three samples at temperature 0.3, and a required JSON
probability triple. It covers the first 1,104 matches of 2025/26 and predates
the complete-season refresh. The experiment is retained as a separately
labelled subset analysis rather than silently extrapolated to the 648 newly
available matches.

\subsection{Validation-selected market anchoring}

The learned and market probabilities are combined as
\begin{equation}
 p_w(c\mid x)=w p_f(c\mid x)+(1-w)p_m(c\mid x),\qquad w\in[0,1].
 \label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
The weight is selected from $\{0,0.05,\ldots,1\}$ by minimum log loss on a
chronologically prior tuning block. Thus $w=0$ is a legitimate outcome: an AI
model is rejected from the probability layer when it does not improve the
external prior. This gate prevents downstream uncertainty machinery from
hiding weak base probabilities.

\subsection{Marginal and class-conditional LAC}

For a labelled calibration observation $(x_i,y_i)$, the least-ambiguous
classification (LAC) nonconformity score is
\begin{equation}
 s_i=1-p_w(y_i\mid x_i).
\end{equation}
Given $n$ calibration scores, the finite-sample quantile index is
$k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$, clipped to $[1,n]$. Marginal LAC uses one
quantile $q$ and returns
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma(x)=\{c:1-p_w(c\mid x)\le q\}.
\end{equation}
Class-conditional (Mondrian) LAC instead estimates $q_c$ from calibration
examples with $y_i=c$ and returns
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_M(x)=\{c:1-p_w(c\mid x)\le q_c\}.
 \label{eq:mondrian}
\end{equation}
The class-specific construction is expected to protect draws from the
undercoverage that can be masked by dominant home-win cases.

\subsection{Match-day adaptive controller}

Fixed $q_c$ values may become stale as the season evolves. The proposed
controller maintains a class-specific effective miscoverage level
$\alpha_{t,c}$, initialized at the target $\alpha$. Before a match day, each
threshold is recomputed from all class-$c$ scores observed up to that date at
quantile $1-\alpha_{t,c}$. After the complete day's outcomes are available,
only the realized class is updated:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{t+1,c}=\operatorname{clip}\left[
 \alpha_{t,c}+\gamma\{\alpha-e_{t,c}\},\ 0.01,\ 0.50\right],
 \label{eq:aci}
\end{equation}
where $e_{t,c}=1$ if the true class $c=y_t$ was excluded and zero otherwise.
Classes not observed in the update retain their previous value. All completed
scores are then appended to their class histories. Matches sharing a date are
processed as a batch: none of their outcomes can affect another prediction on
that date.

The gain parameter is selected using target seasons 2022/23--2024/25 as a
development period. The primary criterion is mean absolute deviation from the
desired coverage, with class-coverage gap used as a secondary criterion. This
selects $\gamma=0.02$ from $\{0,0.005,0.01,0.02,0.05\}$. The case $\gamma=0$
is an expanding-calibration ablation: new scores enter the history, but error
feedback does not alter the nominal level.

\subsection{Module admission and LLM audit}

Set size determines automation but does not prove that a reviewer is useful.
For the LLM subset, three comparisons are made within every triage stratum:
the market argmax, the unrestricted LLM argmax, and the LLM argmax restricted
to labels inside the conformal set. Exact McNemar tests compare paired errors.
The LLM is admitted as a review module only if it improves the anchor on the
same cases. This criterion distinguishes model availability from model value.

\section{Experimental Design}

\subsection{Rolling temporal protocol}

For each target season $t\in\{2022/23,2023/24,2024/25,2025/26\}$, the outcome
model is trained through $t-3$, early stopping uses $t-2$, and season $t-1$ is
split chronologically in half. Its first half selects $w$ in
Equation~\ref{eq:blend}; its second half calibrates conformal scores. Season
$t$ is then processed in date order. The experiment is repeated with random
seeds 7, 42, and 2026. The final fixed predictive comparison uses five seeds
(7, 42, 101, 2026, and 31415).

\subsection{Comparators and metrics}

The pipeline that produces every number and figure in this article is publicly
available at
\url{https://github.com/wenjin06/football-outcome-forecasting-benchmark},
including the processed feature matrix, the calibration and evaluation scripts,
and the raw result files from which the tables are generated.

Prediction-set comparators are marginal LAC, fixed Mondrian-LAC, expanding
Mondrian-LAC ($\gamma=0$), and the adaptive controller. APS and RAPS were also
implemented as diagnostic baselines. In this three-class task, at 90\% target
coverage their mean sets contain approximately 2.8--3.0 labels and virtually
never yield a singleton, so they are reported as an inefficiency diagnostic
rather than used for routing.

Point predictions are evaluated by accuracy and multiclass log loss.
Prediction sets are evaluated by marginal coverage, H/D/A coverage, maximum
class-coverage gap, mean set size, action rates, and singleton accuracy.
Accuracy and log-loss differences use paired observations. Exact McNemar tests
compare classifications. Confidence intervals use 5,000 bootstrap replicates
over league-by-ISO-week blocks, retaining local temporal and competition
dependence. Financial simulations are secondary stress tests: one-unit bets
use the predicted argmax and Bet365 closing odds. They do not represent an
executable pre-closing strategy because the same closing information appears
in the inputs.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Learned models do not improve the market probabilities}

\input{tables/table1_predictive}

Table~\ref{tab:predictive} answers the first research question. The complete
2025/26 market baseline attains 53.3\% accuracy and 0.978 log loss. The
five-seed XGBoost ensemble differs by only +0.06 percentage points in accuracy
(95\% CI $-0.90$ to $+1.04$), with exact McNemar $p=1.000$. The random-forest
ensemble differs by +0.11 points (CI $-1.01$ to $+1.26$; $p=0.920$). Both
ensembles have slightly worse log loss than the market. Across the four rolling
experiments, the mean validation-selected model weight is 0.004; eleven of
twelve season--seed runs select $w=0$, and one selects $w=0.05$. Subsequent
results therefore concern risk control built predominantly on the strongest
validated probability source, not an asserted machine-learning edge.

The complete-season ablation in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:ablation} supports the
same interpretation. Removing market variables reduces accuracy from 52.9\%
to 51.4\% and worsens log loss from 0.983 to 1.004. xG, form, rank, and referee
groups yield smaller and inconsistent changes. Market variables account for
approximately 89\% of positive aggregate permutation importance; referee
importance is slightly negative.

\subsection{Adaptive class-conditional calibration tracks the target}

\input{tables/table3_conformal_rolling}

At the 90\% operating point, marginal LAC attains 91.8\% average coverage but
has a 9.73-point mean gap between the best- and worst-covered outcomes
(Table~\ref{tab:conformal-rolling}). Fixed Mondrian calibration reduces the
gap to 4.40 points and absolute target error to 0.95 points. Merely appending
new scores ($\gamma=0$) improves both metrics, showing that recent observations
matter. Adaptive feedback with $\gamma=0.02$ reduces mean target error to 0.14
points and the class gap to 0.67 points, with a mean set size of 2.334. The
pattern is visible across all four target seasons in Figure~\ref{fig:rolling}.

\subsection{Coverage controls the automation workload}

\input{tables/table4_operating_points}

Table~\ref{tab:operating} reports the operational trade-off. At a 90\% target,
15.7\% of matches are automated at 74.9\% accuracy, 31.8\% produce a two-label
review set, and 52.5\% retain all three outcomes. Reducing target coverage to
80\% raises automation to 28.7\% at 70.6\% accuracy. At 70\%, 43.1\% are
automated at 63.0\% accuracy and only 2.0\% retain the full set. Figure
\ref{fig:triage} shows that coverage has a direct and interpretable workload
meaning; it is not merely a calibration statistic.

\input{tables/table5_paired_conformal}

The paired final-season analysis in Table~\ref{tab:paired-conformal} separates
efficiency from sampling noise. Relative to fixed Mondrian calibration, the
adaptive controller reduces mean set size by 0.048 labels (95\% CI
0.031--0.065) and raises the singleton automation rate by 2.80 percentage
points (1.85--3.78). Its singleton-accuracy difference is $-2.10$ points, but
the interval ($-5.16$ to $+0.73$) crosses zero. The target-error and class-gap
improvements over fixed Mondrian also have intervals crossing zero within this
single season; their stronger support comes from the repeated direction and
smaller average deviations over four rolling seasons. Relative to marginal
LAC, class-gap and set-size reductions are clearly positive, while marginal
LAC automates slightly more cases by tolerating unequal class protection.

\subsection{The LLM does not resolve conformal ambiguity}

\input{tables/table6_llm_triage}

The 1,104-match LLM subset provides a direct module-admission test
(Table~\ref{tab:llm-triage}). Overall market and LLM accuracies are 54.17\% and
53.89\%; only nine cases are discordant in correctness, and exact McNemar
$p=0.508$. In the singleton stratum, both are correct on 81.2\% of 144 matches.
In the two-label review stratum, both attain 53.2\% on 361 matches; constraining
the LLM to its preferred label inside the set changes nothing. In the full-set
stratum, the market reaches 48.2\% and the LLM 47.7\%. The LLM therefore adds
no measurable information where the risk controller requests review. Its
probabilities remain useful as an example of a candidate module that fails a
predefined admission test.

\subsection{Financial and draw diagnostics}

Completion of the target season materially changes the financial result. The
XGBoost all-bet return is $-1.93$\%, and the earlier UI-tier policy returns
$-1.44$\%. Value selection loses at every tested threshold
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:value}), from $-7.89$\% at EV $>0$ to $-16.00$\% at
EV $>0.15$. Isotonic calibration reduces some losses but does not create an
edge. These results are consistent with the predictive comparison and show why
a partial-season positive return is not an adequate contribution claim.

Draws remain the main discriminative bottleneck. Their empirical rate is
25.5\%, but XGBoost chooses a draw as argmax for only 3.0\% of matches. The
draw probability is reasonably calibrated (class ECE 0.010) yet weakly
resolved: its Brier resolution is 0.0026, compared with 0.0344 for home wins,
and its correlation with the draw indicator is 0.117. Class weighting raises
draw recall but reduces overall accuracy and worsens log loss. Conformal class
conditioning addresses a different problem: it does not force more draw point
predictions; it prevents the draw label from being systematically excluded
from uncertainty sets.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the framework adds}

The findings support a separation between probability generation and decision
risk. When a strong external prior exists, adding model complexity is not
automatically beneficial. The validation-selected anchor makes this explicit:
the pipeline can assign zero weight to a learned model and still use modern
uncertainty quantification to determine which outputs are sufficiently narrow
for automation. This property is useful beyond betting markets wherever an
external consensus, expert score, or operational baseline already aggregates
substantial information.

The adaptive class-conditional controller adds a second form of discipline.
Marginal coverage alone makes the system appear conservative while masking
large outcome differences. Fixed class conditioning improves balance, but its
thresholds drift between seasons. Updating after each completed match day
produces near-target prequential coverage without using a future outcome or
another match result from the same day. The $\gamma=0$ ablation shows that
simply accumulating more calibration scores explains part, but not all, of the
improvement.

Prediction-set size also supplies an intelligible interface. The target
coverage chosen by a system owner determines how much workload is automated,
reviewed, or rejected. No claim is made that a doubleton automatically improves
a human decision; that would require observations from actual reviewers. The
LLM experiment instead shows how a proposed reviewer can be audited. In the
present data it fails to add value, so promoting it as an ambiguity resolver
would be unsupported.

\subsection{Implications for AI forecasting research}

Three evaluation practices follow. First, all candidate models should be
compared with the strongest deployable prior under identical information and
settlement rules. Second, uncertainty should be assessed through coverage,
set efficiency, and downstream routing rather than through a hand-designed
score alone. Third, a second-stage AI component must be evaluated conditionally
on the cases it will actually receive. Aggregate LLM accuracy cannot establish
value in a review workflow.

The experiment also distinguishes forecast confidence from financial value.
Singleton cases are substantially more accurate, but they often correspond to
short-priced favourites whose certainty is already reflected in the odds.
Accordingly, higher selective accuracy does not imply positive expected return.
The paper treats closing-line ROI as a descriptive stress test, not a claim of
profitability.

\subsection{Limitations}

The conformal interpretation is bounded by temporal dependence. Standard
finite-sample validity assumes exchangeability, which cannot be guaranteed
across football seasons. Adaptive updates substantially improve empirical
prequential coverage, but they do not prove distribution-free validity under
arbitrary drift. Formal analysis for clustered schedules and interacting team
states remains open.

The evidence covers men's football in five European top divisions and one
three-way pre-match market. Results may not transfer to lower divisions,
in-play markets, handicap markets, or other sports. The settlement protocol
uses one bookmaker's closing prices, while a deployable strategy would need an
earlier cutoff and would incur taxes, limits, slippage, and latency.

Textual news and player-availability data are excluded because reliable
timestamped coverage was not available uniformly across leagues. Adding text
would require article-level publication times, a strict cutoff before kickoff,
retrieval logs, and missing-news coverage analysis. The LLM subset ends in
February 2026 and is therefore smaller than the completed test season. A full
rerun could tighten its interval but is unlikely to alter the observed
near-identity with market probabilities; it should be undertaken only as a
pre-specified replication.

Finally, ``review'' is a routing label, not an observed human outcome. A future
prospective study should recruit analysts, randomize whether they receive a
point prediction or conformal set, measure time and accuracy, and test whether
LLM-generated rationales help or induce automation bias.

\section{Conclusion}

This study reframed football forecasting as a risk-controlled decision problem
rather than a contest for marginal point-accuracy gains. On the complete
2025/26 season, five-seed tree ensembles did not significantly improve on
de-vigged closing probabilities, and validation-selected blending almost
always rejected their incremental contribution. Class-conditional conformal
sets nevertheless provided a useful operational layer. Match-day adaptive
feedback kept home, draw, and away coverage close to common targets across
four rolling seasons and converted those targets into explicit automation,
review, and abstention workloads. An existing LLM failed to improve the market
in any stratum, demonstrating that a trustworthy AI system may derive value
from knowing when not to add another model. The resulting framework offers a
reproducible route from calibrated probabilities to selective decisions under
a strong external prior.

\paragraph{Adaptation sensitivity.}
On the 2022/23--2024/25 development seasons at 90\% target coverage, mean
absolute coverage errors for $\gamma=0$, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, and 0.05 are 0.71,
0.18, 0.16, 0.12, and 0.34 percentage points. Corresponding mean class gaps
are 4.10, 2.07, 1.04, 0.73, and 0.42 points. The primary choice
$\gamma=0.02$ minimizes the first criterion while remaining close to the best
class-balance result. On final 2025/26, its target error is 0.22 points and its
class gap 0.48 points.

\paragraph{APS/RAPS diagnostic.}
At the 90\% operating point on 2025/26, deterministic APS and RAPS sets based
on model probabilities have marginal coverage near 100\% and mean size 2.999.
Market-based APS/RAPS sets attain approximately 98.9\% coverage with mean size
2.958. Their Mondrian variants reduce mean size only to about 2.78 and yield
virtually no singletons. LAC is therefore the more useful set construction for
the three-class routing objective; this is an empirical efficiency result, not
a general claim about APS or RAPS.
